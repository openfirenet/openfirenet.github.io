Home Assistant

The integration adds your stove to Home Assistant as a thermostat, with its fans, switches, sensors and diagnostics. It talks to the bridge on your network: no cloud.

Using another home automation system (Jeedom, openHAB, Node-RED, Domoticz...)? See MQTT. If you would rather not install an integration, the bridge can also announce the stove to Home Assistant through MQTT discovery, with fewer features.

Before you start

  • A bridge running the Open Firenet firmware, linked to the stove and reachable on your network (see Get started).
  • Home Assistant 2024.1 or later.
  • HACS, recommended for installation and updates.

Install with HACS

  1. In HACS, open Integrations, then the menu at the top right, then Custom repositories.

  2. Add https://github.com/openfirenet/open-firenet-ha with the category Integration.

  3. Search for Open Firenet and click Download.

  4. Restart Home Assistant.

Without HACS: copy the folder custom_components/open_firenet/ from the repository into your Home Assistant config/custom_components/ folder and restart.

Add your stove

  1. Go to Settings, Devices & services, Add integration.

  2. Search for Open Firenet.

  3. Enter the address of the bridge (open-firenet.local or its IP address) and the refresh interval (30 seconds by default). A name given by your router only works once you have added it on the bridge's page (Bridge tab, section "Access to the page").

Heating schedule

The stove keeps its own weekly schedule: two time ranges per day, during which it heats. Since version 2.6 of the integration you fill it from Home Assistant, by drawing your week on Home Assistant's schedule grid and copying it to the stove. The schedule then runs in the stove itself: it keeps working when Home Assistant or the Wi-Fi is down.

1. Draw your week

  1. In Home Assistant, open Settings > Devices & services > Helpers and press Create helper.
  2. Choose Schedule and give it a name, for example "Stove".
  3. On the weekly grid, drag on a day to draw a time range. Click a range to set its exact start and end, or to delete it.
  4. Draw at most two ranges per day, which is what the stove can hold. A day left empty means no heating range that day.

2. Tell the integration which schedule to use

  1. Open Settings > Devices & services > Open Firenet and press Configure.
  2. Under "Schedule to copy to the stove", choose the schedule you created, and submit.

This is done once.

3. Copy it to the stove

  1. Open the stove's device page (Settings > Devices & services > Open Firenet, then the device).
  2. In the "Configuration" block, press Copy schedule to the stove.

The whole week is sent in one command and replaces what the stove held, empty days included. You can check the result on the bridge's page, Stove tab, "Schedule".

4. Switch the schedule on

The copy fills the stove's schedule; the switch Heating Schedule decides whether the stove follows it. Switch it on from the device page, or from an automation.

After each change of the grid, press the button again. The copy is not automatic: the stove keeps the last schedule it received.

Good to know

  • More than two ranges in a day: the copy is refused with a message naming the day, and nothing is sent to the stove.
  • A range that ends at midnight is sent as ending at 23:59.
  • To clear the stove's schedule: empty the grid and press the button.
  • The Home Assistant schedule itself switches nothing. It only serves as the drawing board; its own on/off state is not used.
  • For automations, each of the stove's slots also exists as a start and an end time entity, with "Send schedule to the stove" and "Discard schedule changes" buttons. They are disabled by default: enable the ones you need on the device page. A changed time is kept in Home Assistant until the send button is pressed.

Stove without a RIKA room sensor

If your stove has no room sensor, its room temperature is unknown. You can show the temperature of any Home Assistant sensor on the thermostat instead: open the integration's Configure dialog and pick the sensor.

This only changes what the thermostat displays. The stove keeps regulating with its own sensor. To switch the stove on and off from another sensor, use Home Assistant's Generic Thermostat or an automation.

Problems

If the entities are unavailable, first check that http://open-firenet.local opens in your browser and shows the stove as connected. Then see Troubleshooting, or open an issue on the integration's repository.
