Troubleshooting

Start with the bridge's own page at http://open-firenet.local: since version 3.2 it tells you why there is no link with the stove.

The stove is not connected

Banner: "The bridge is not connected to the stove over USB"

The stove does not see the bridge at all. Check, in this order:

  • The USB port on the board. On boards with two USB ports, the stove must be on the one labelled "USB" or "USB OTG", not "UART" or "COM".
  • The cable. It must carry data. Many short cables only carry power.
  • The stove. It must be powered: its USB socket powers the board.
Banner: "The stove does not answer"

The stove sees the bridge but has not sent anything. Switch the stove off at the mains for a minute, then on again, and wait two minutes. If nothing changes, download the log (Diagnostics tab) and report it.

The stove restarts when the bridge is plugged in

The board probably draws too much current from the stove's USB socket when it starts. It is uncommon; it was seen with a stick that has a screen. Put a USB extension cable between the stove and the board: see Hardware.

The stove screen shows "FIRENET UPDATE" or a code UW29 (or another UW code)

Both come from the stove's check of the stick: "FIRENET UPDATE" means it judged the stick's version out of date and offers to update it, UW29 that it did not get a valid identification in time. Do not confirm the update on the stove. Update the bridge instead: versions before 3.6.1 could fail this check on stoves with firmware 2.28. If it persists on the latest version, report it with the log.

The page says "Waiting for the stove" and the values are greyed out

The bridge has not received any data from the stove yet. Right after power-up this is normal for a few seconds. If it stays, read the banner above it.

The page cannot be reached

http://open-firenet.local does not open
  • Some networks and Android phones do not resolve .local names. Use the IP address instead: the installer's Network Scan tab shows it, and so does your router.
  • Your device must be on the same network as the bridge.
The page says "The bridge does not answer under this name"

Since firmware 4.0 the bridge only answers at its IP address and at open-firenet.local. If you open it under another name, for example the one your router gives it (open-firenet.lan, open-firenet.fritz.box) or a domain of your own, its page shows this message with a link to its IP address.

  • Follow the link, open the Bridge tab, section "Access to the page", and add the name you use (one per line, 8 at most). It then works under that name too, at once.
  • A name must contain a dot. Adding example.org also accepts stove.example.org.

This is what keeps a page of another website, opened in a browser on your network, from reading or commanding the bridge.

The bridge is not on my network any more

When it cannot join your Wi-Fi, the bridge goes to setup mode and creates the network Open-Firenet-Setup. Join it and enter your Wi-Fi again. Only 2.4 GHz networks are supported.

Since firmware 4.0 the bridge rejoins your network by itself: after a drop it keeps trying, and when it starts before your router (after a power cut) it leaves setup mode as soon as the network answers. With an earlier firmware it stays off the network until it is restarted: unplug it and plug it back, then update.

Since firmware 4.0, when a Wi-Fi password is set the bridge only joins an encrypted network (WPA or later): it no longer joins an open network, or one still protected by the old WEP, that carries the same name.

The bridge often loses the Wi-Fi, or its page is slow to answer

Since firmware 4.0, the Bridge tab has a "Wi-Fi power saving" switch. It is on by default: the bridge runs cooler and draws less power, and an answer can wait up to a quarter of a second. Switch it off if the bridge often loses the Wi-Fi or if you want it to answer faster; the change applies at once.

The "Bridge health" card of the Diagnostics tab counts the Wi-Fi disconnections since the last start, which tells whether the change helps.

Updates

The wireless update times out

A wireless update needs the computer and the bridge to reach each other both ways: the installer sends an invitation to the bridge (UDP port 3232), then the bridge connects back to the computer to download the firmware. On networks with separate segments (VLANs, guest Wi-Fi) or with a firewall on the computer, this is blocked.

  • Put the computer on the same network as the bridge, or
  • allow incoming connections from the local network in the computer's firewall, or
  • update over USB instead.
The firmware is too large for a wireless update

A bridge first flashed from the Arduino IDE with the default partition scheme has update slots of 1,310,720 bytes. Firmware 3.5.0 and later is larger than that: the bridge refuses the update without a word and the installer times out. Installer 1.6.0 names this cause, and refuses at once when the bridge runs firmware 3.6.0 or later.

  • Flash the bridge once over USB with the installer, in Full Reset (Factory Image) mode. It is the only mode that rewrites the partition table: the default mode, Update (Keep Configuration), leaves the old one in place. You will have to enter your Wi-Fi again, and wireless updates work afterwards.
  • If you build from source in the Arduino IDE, select Tools > Partition Scheme > "Minimal SPIFFS (1.9MB APP with OTA)".

A bridge flashed with the installer is not concerned.

After updating to firmware 4.0, something stopped working

Firmware 4.0 closes the bridge to pages of other websites. Three things change:

  • Home Assistant shows the stove as unavailable, or the page stays empty: the bridge was set up or opened under a name given by your router. Use its IP address or open-firenet.local, or add the name on the bridge's page (see "The bridge does not answer under this name" above). Update the Home Assistant integration to 2.6.0 or later: it tells you this in plain words.
  • A dashboard of your own, served from another address, no longer reads the bridge from the browser: the bridge no longer allows requests from other pages. Call it from a program instead (a script, Home Assistant, Node-RED), or use MQTT.
  • A script that restarted the bridge with a GET request: /api/restart now needs a POST.
The installer asks for an update password, or I forgot it

Since firmware 4.0 you can protect wireless updates with a password. It is set, or removed, with the installer while the bridge is plugged into your computer (Wi-Fi tab, "Update password"), never over the network. The installer then asks for it at each wireless update and does not keep it.

If you forgot it: flash the bridge once over USB in "Full Reset (Factory Image)" mode. That removes the password, and you enter your Wi-Fi again.

The version did not change after an update

Check the version next to the title on the bridge's page. With a weak Wi-Fi signal an update can fail silently; update over USB with the installer.

Values

The room temperature shows "--"

Your stove has no RIKA room sensor. In Home Assistant you can display another sensor on the thermostat, see Home Assistant.

The air flaps value looks ten times smaller than on the stove screen

The stove screen shows the air flaps in per mille (‰), the bridge in percent: 50 ‰ on the screen is 5 % on the page.

I changed a MultiAir setting and the stove screen still shows the old value

The setting is applied at once, but we have seen on several firmware versions that the stove's own screen does not refresh its MultiAir menu while it is open: leave the menu and open it again, and the new value is there. Other settings, such as the heating power, refresh on the screen right away.

The Diagnostics tab

The Diagnostics tab of the bridge's page (http://open-firenet.local/#diagnostics) gathers what is needed to understand a problem: the health of the bridge itself (cause of its last restart, memory, Wi-Fi, how many times the link with the stove was established and lost), the state of the link, the log of the exchanges, and every value the stove reports. The "Diagnostic file" button puts all of it in one file to attach to a report.

MQTT

The MQTT badge says "Refused: wrong user or password"

The broker rejected the bridge. Enter the password again in the Bridge tab and save: the field is empty by design, and shows "Unchanged" once a password is stored.

The MQTT badge says "Broker unreachable"

Check the broker address and port (1883 by default), and that the broker accepts connections from your network. The bridge retries every 10 seconds; the link with the stove is not affected.

The MQTT badge says "Certificate not trusted"

TLS is on, and the bridge refuses the broker's certificate. If your broker has a self-made certificate, paste the certificate of the authority that signed it in the MQTT card: see MQTT, encrypted connection. Without it, only certificates signed by a public authority, for the name entered as broker address, are accepted.

The MQTT badge says "Secure connection failed"

The TLS dialogue with the broker failed before the certificate was even checked. The usual cause is TLS ticked on the broker's plain port: TLS is normally on port 8883, plain MQTT on 1883.

The stove appears twice in Home Assistant

The Open Firenet integration and MQTT discovery are both active. Keep one: untick "Home Assistant discovery" in the MQTT card of the Bridge tab (its entities are then removed), or remove the integration.

I ticked "Home Assistant discovery" and nothing appears

Check that "Enable MQTT" is ticked too and that the badge says "Connected", and that Home Assistant's own MQTT integration is set up on the same broker.

A command sent over MQTT is ignored

Commands are ignored while the stove is not linked, during the few seconds after the link comes up while the bridge does not know the stove's settings yet, and when they are published with the retain flag. See MQTT.

Report a problem

Open an issue with:

  • the stove model and its firmware version (Info menu of the stove);
  • the bridge firmware version (next to the title on its page);
  • the diagnostic file: Diagnostics tab, "Diagnostic file", taken one to two minutes after switching the stove on, or right after the problem shows;
  • what the stove screen shows, with any code.

The diagnostic file holds the bridge health, the link, every value and the exchange log. Your Wi-Fi name and the bridge's MAC address are masked in it, and the Wi-Fi password is never included.

On bridge firmware 3.6.1 and older there is no such button: send the output of http://open-firenet.local/api/state and the log (Diagnostics tab, "Download" in the exchange log).
